## English paragraph (Results-ready)

To connect hepatic inflammation with peripheral pain signaling in human tissue, we profiled receptor and ion-channel expression across 1,837 nuclei from six human dorsal root ganglion (DRG) donors, partitioned into 15 neuronal clusters. The sensory neurons did not express the inflammatory enzymes and cytokines identified in the liver: PTGS2 and IL1B were detected in 0.0% of nuclei, and IL6 in 0.05%. The same neurons nevertheless carried the receptors that detect these mediators. The prostaglandin E2 receptor PTGER3 (EP3) was the dominant prostaglandin receptor, expressed in 30.0% of neurons across clusters and in 83.9% of the A-delta peptidergic nociceptor cluster H3, whereas PTGER1 was nearly absent (0.1%). IL6ST (gp130) was present in 59.3% of neurons while the specific IL6R reached only 7.6%, consistent with IL-6 trans-signaling; TNFRSF1A and NTRK1 occurred in 15.6% and 33.8% of neurons. Voltage-gated sodium channels SCN9A, SCN10A, and SCN11A together with TRPV1 and TRPA1 were expressed in the same nociceptor clusters, providing the machinery that converts receptor activation into action potentials. These results separate the two sides of the pathway in human cells: immune cells in the inflamed liver produce the mediators, and nociceptors carry the matching receptors and the downstream excitability machinery. The analysis establishes molecular compatibility between hepatic inflammatory signaling and human pain pathways. It does not test whether the formula changes these receptors or produces analgesia, and transcript levels cannot determine the net signaling direction of EP3, which requires functional assays.
